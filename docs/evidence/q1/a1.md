# Q1 — Use case design

## Context

This use case focuses on a boutique asset manager based in Barcelona, 50-200 workers, approximately €4B assets under management, operating under CNMV supervision. The firm runs its own internal research team, publishes investment recommendations to clients, and is fully authorised under MiFID II, meaning it faces the full weight of EU financial regulation. Their issue is that analysts spend too much time manually searching through fund prospectuses, regulatory filings, Bloomberg terminals, internal research notes, etc. to answer client questions or prepare investment reports. This is slow, costly in analyst hours, and introduces risk. A missed regulatory update or an outdated internal view can translate into a bad client recommendation. Therefore using a self-hosted AI allows the company to control everything. From who logs in (Casdoor) and what files are stored (MinIO) to what internal documents the AI can search (Qdrant), what external tools the AI can call (MCPHub), and how documents get processed into searchable knowledge (Hayhooks).

## Personas

**Research Analyst:** Before the AI, the analyst spends 3-4 hours per investment memo manually searching through filings, cross-referencing internal research, and pulling Bloomberg data. The pain point is not just time but consistency. Two analysts researching the same stock will find different documents depending on what they happen to search for. This inconsistency creates reputational and regulatory risk when two client-facing recommendations are based on different underlying research. With the stack, RAG over Qdrant ensures every analyst searches the same indexed corpus every time.

**Relationship Manager:** Before the AI, the RM puts clients on hold during calls to open Bloomberg or email an analyst for a quick position lookup. This creates friction and looks unprofessional. With the stack, the RM gets an answer in under 3 seconds without leaving the conversation.

**Compliance Officer:** Before the AI, the compliance officer manually reviews email threads and call recordings to audit investment advice. With the stack, every AI interaction is logged in Postgres with the Casdoor user identity attached, making audit retrieval a query rather than a manual search.

## Journey

**Upload time (happens once per document):**

1. Analyst uploads ESMA regulatory filing PDF into LobeChat chat interface
2. MinIO stores the file in the internal S3-compatible bucket
3. Hayhooks pipeline triggers automatically, chunks the PDF, embeds it, stores vectors in Qdrant
4. Document is now permanently searchable without ever entering a prompt again

**Query time, Analyst workflow:**

5. Analyst logs in via Casdoor SSO (same credentials as company email, session logged to Postgres)
6. Asks: "Summarize our internal research on Iberdrola and compare with the latest ESMA filing"
7. Qdrant fires, semantic RAG search over both the internal research notes and the previously ingested ESMA filing (both embedded into the same vector store at upload time), returns top 5 relevant passages
8. Claude Sonnet synthesizes both sources into a structured summary (multi-document synthesis, long context required)
9. Analyst asks: "What is our current exposure to Iberdrola vs the mandate limit?"
10. MCPHub filesystem tool fires, reads OMS-exported portfolio CSV from shared volume, returns exposure figure and flags if near mandate limit (Claude Sonnet)
11. Analyst uploads final investment memo to MinIO directly from chat

**Query time, Relationship Manager workflow:**

12. RM is on a live client call, opens LobeChat
13. Asks: "What is client X current allocation to European equities?"
14. MCPHub filesystem tool fires, reads portfolio CSV
15. Claude Haiku returns answer in under 3 seconds (fast retrieval, no deep reasoning needed)
16. RM answers client without putting them on hold or opening Bloomberg

**Compliance Officer workflow:**

17. End of week, compliance officer opens LobeChat admin interface
18. Asks: "Show me all conversations where the word recommendation appeared this week"
19. LobeChat admin interface retrieves conversation logs from Postgres with Casdoor user identity attached to each message
20. Claude Haiku summarizes and flags relevant entries from what the interface returns
21. Officer exports flagged conversations as PDF, submits to head of compliance with Casdoor user identity as metadata (Claude Haiku)

**Model routing:**

- Analyst heavy analysis: Claude Sonnet/Opus
- RM client Q&A: Claude Haiku
- Compliance review: Claude Haiku

## Why this stack vs ChatGPT/Copilot

By having a self-hosted AI, data sovereignty relies on the company itself. In the case of ChatGPT the data sovereignty falls onto OpenAI servers. MiFID II Article 16 requires investment firms to retain client-related communications under their own control. If chat logs live on OpenAI's servers, you don't fully control retrieval when a regulator demands it. GDPR Article 28 means client PII leaving your perimeter requires a Data Processing Agreement, which most EU compliance teams will not approve regardless. DORA, in force since January 2025, requires EU financial entities to document, monitor, and maintain an exit plan for every critical ICT third-party dependency. If OpenAI goes down, changes pricing, or gets acquired, the firm needs a documented exit plan and evidence of ongoing monitoring. Self-hosting eliminates OpenAI as a critical third-party entirely, so there is nothing to register, monitor, or exit.

Beyond regulatory compliance, ChatGPT cannot access years of internal research notes. Uploading per session doesn't work for a continuously growing data corpus. You can't paste 10 years of memos into a context window each time. RAG over Qdrant makes the entire corpus permanently searchable without ever being in the prompt. This is a persistent searchable knowledge base, not a one-time context upload.

Then there is the per-assistant configuration which allows each persona to have a tailored assistant with its own default model, enabled tools, and system prompt. The analyst gets Claude Sonnet with Qdrant and Hayhooks enabled, the RM gets Claude Haiku with just the portfolio filesystem tool, and the compliance officer gets a read-only log view with Haiku. No manual setup per user, no risk of an RM accidentally running an expensive Opus query on a simple portfolio lookup. ChatGPT gives everyone the same interface and the same model.

ChatGPT Enterprise offers a higher uptime SLA and mobile app support that this stack currently lacks, but for a regulated buy-side firm the audit and sovereignty requirements outweigh convenience features.

A boutique fund in Barcelona operating under CNMV supervision cannot afford a regulatory finding caused by an AI tool it does not control. This stack makes the AI auditable, not just useful.
